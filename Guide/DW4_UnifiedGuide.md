# Dragon Warrior IV Unified Walkthrough

This guide is a section-by-section route for the North American NES release of Dragon Warrior IV in a RetroAchievements-focused playthrough. It uses the original NES localization, mechanics, maps, equipment, and five-chapter structure; remake-only names and content are outside its scope. The goal is to keep the main story path, chapter-specific objectives, optional treasures, party routing, and achievement timing in the same place so each achievement appears near the moment it can naturally be earned.

The achievement names, descriptions, and points come from RetroAchievements game 4612, whose saved page is titled *Dragon Quest IV: Michibikareshi Monotachi* for NES/Famicom and whose saved code notes use Japanese-layout addresses. The walkthrough directions use North American NES names and gameplay data; the overlay reports ROM region and evidence source separately. This guide does not claim compatibility for translation patches or the PlayStation, Nintendo DS, iOS, or Android remakes.

## Introduction

Dragon Warrior IV replaces the single continuous opening of the earlier games with four smaller adventures and a fifth chapter that brings their heroes together. Those opening chapters are not disposable prologues: they establish the permanent cast, determine the levels and equipment each character brings into Chapter 5, and introduce places that the united party will revisit later.

### Story So Far

Dragon Warrior IV begins a new continuity unconnected to the Erdrick trilogy of the first three games. Its different world establishes the Zenithian legend, where scattered kingdoms face a threat they do not yet understand and rumors point toward a Hero named in prophecy.

The first four chapters follow the people whose lives will eventually converge around that Hero. Ragnar, a royal soldier of Burland, investigates the disappearance of local children. Princess Alena escapes the restrictions of Santeem Castle to test her strength, accompanied by Cristo and Brey. Taloon leaves his family in Lakanaba to pursue his dream of owning a shop and becoming the world's greatest merchant. In Monbaraba, the sisters Nara and Mara begin a search for the man responsible for their father's death.

Chapter 5 begins with the Hero and turns those separate journeys into one expedition. The earlier protagonists must be found again, the wagon filled, the Zenithian equipment assembled, and the force behind the spreading evil confronted. The guide will preserve each chapter's perspective as it happens rather than revealing later connections before the route reaches them.

### Chapter and Party Overview

Each opening chapter has its own party, economy, and immediate objective. Permanent characters keep their levels and carried equipment when they return in Chapter 5, but loose gold from the solo chapters does not carry forward. Before ending Chapters 1-4, spend surplus gold on useful equipment or items that can be retained and sold later rather than leaving it unused.

| Chapter | Lead and Party | Route Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Ragnar, with Healie as a temporary companion | Build Ragnar into a durable front-line fighter while solving the disappearances around Burland and Izmit. |
| 2 | Alena / Cristo / Brey | Develop a fast physical attacker, a healer, and an offensive-support mage while traveling from Santeem to the Endor Tournament. |
| 3 | Taloon, with optional hired help | Build Taloon's fortune, secure his shop, and fund the tunnel that links Endor and Branca. |
| 4 | Nara / Mara, with Orin as a temporary companion | Develop a support healer and offensive mage while pursuing Balzack from Monbaraba to Keeleon. |
| 5 | Hero, eventually joined by the seven earlier protagonists | Reunite the chosen ones, manage the wagon and tactics system, collect the Zenithian equipment, and prepare multiple endgame parties. |

Once the permanent cast is reunited, do not treat Chapter 5 as a four-character campaign. The standard party can receive the best equipment first, but the restricted Necrosaro achievements require viable alternatives to the Hero. Keep the non-Hero front line and healers equipped well enough to function when called out of the wagon.

| Character | Primary Role | Long-Term Route Value |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hero | Directly controlled physical attacker, emergency healer, and utility caster | Anchors the standard route and can correct decisions the AI-controlled party members will not make. |
| Ragnar | High-HP, high-strength front-line fighter | Durable physical damage for bosses and a leading candidate for the no-Hero party. |
| Alena | Fast physical attacker with frequent critical hits | Strong boss damage and one of the best answers to King Metal encounters. |
| Cristo | Durable healing and defensive support | Important sustained healing for the main route and restricted final-party attempts. |
| Brey | Ice offense, utility magic, and Bikill support | Can accelerate physical parties when the AI chooses the right support action. |
| Taloon | Physical fighter with unpredictable special actions | A durable reserve whose free-form actions can help or hinder depending on the fight. |
| Nara | Healing and defensive support | Gives the no-Hero roster another source of recovery, though her lower durability requires protection. |
| Mara | Fire offense and BeDragon | Provides reliable magical damage and a tactics-dependent answer to metal enemies. |

Temporary companions such as Healie, Orin, Hector, Lucia, and Doran support particular route segments but do not replace development of the eight permanent characters. Their arrival and departure points will be called out where they occur.

### Chapter 5 Tactics and AI

In Chapter 5, the player directly commands only the Hero. Other permanent party members choose their actions according to the selected tactic and the battle state at the moment their turn begins. They react to damage, healing, and defeated targets caused by earlier allies, but they do not plan around the Hero's queued action. Watch what the party has already done before committing the Hero to healing or targeting an enemy that may be defeated first.

The party also maintains hidden knowledge of each monster's spell resistances. A newly encountered monster begins at knowledge rank 0. Completing rounds against it advances that entry toward rank 3, where the AI has exact resistance knowledge for the rest of the game. Six casts of Ironize cover eighteen rounds and give a 99 percent chance of reaching rank 3 from rank 0. This is excessive for ordinary encounters, but it is a useful training method when a dangerous boss or recurring enemy causes allies to waste turns on resisted spells. Knowledge gained during a failed boss attempt is retained after revival.

| Tactic | Practical Use |
| --- | --- |
| Normal | Balanced default for unfamiliar encounters and most routine travel. |
| Offensive | Prioritizes ending the fight and spends MP more freely; it also encourages Mara to use BeDragon against metal enemies. |
| Defensive | Makes healing and defensive actions more attractive. It also enables Nara to cast Farewell, so use it carefully when her MP is low and allies have fallen. |
| Save MP | Reduces expensive casting and prevents Healus and BeDragon, which can be dangerous when full-party recovery is needed. |
| Use No MP | Restricts allies to attacks and combat-usable items; useful when conserving the last of the party's MP. |
| Try Out | Produces deliberately unpredictable actions and permits certain unusual combat items. It is not an AI-training mode. |

For major bosses, party composition, tactic choice, learned resistance knowledge, and the Hero's inventory matter as much as raw levels. Later boss preparations will specify who should be active, which tactic to begin with, when the Hero must cover healing, and whether an AI-learning setup is worthwhile.

### Game-Long Achievement Plan

Most achievements are earned within one location or story event. The following achievements either span chapters, accumulate over much of Chapter 5, or become substantially harder if preparation is postponed until the final battle.

| Achievement | Points | Long-Range Plan |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Tunnel Rat | 5 | Taloon funds the Endor-Branca tunnel during Chapter 3. Return to and use the completed tunnel in Chapter 5 so the delayed payoff is not skipped. |
| Small Medal Collector | 50 | Track every medal from the first Chapter 5 visit to Konenber onward. Collect both Konenber ship medals before completing the Great Lighthouse because one becomes permanently unavailable afterward. Every later section will maintain a running total toward the required 32. |
| High Roller | 5 | Defer the serious casino grind until Chapter 5. Build a starting coin reserve, use the Endor poker table and double-or-nothing game, and bank progress with normal in-game saves. Hold at least 10,000 coins at once before buying prizes. |
| The One That Didn't Get Away | 5 | Keep Alena and Mara usable for late-game King Metal hunting. Alena's critical hits and Mara's tactics-driven BeDragon provide better routes than ordinary attacks alone. |
| We Don't Need Another Hero | 25 | From the Chapter 5 reunion onward, maintain a non-Hero front line and at least one capable non-Hero healer. The Hero's direct commands will be unavailable for this Necrosaro clear, making tactics and equipment depth essential. |
| Just the Two of Us | 25 | Prepare a literal two-member endgame team instead of relying only on the standard four. Preserve strong defensive equipment and recovery options, and keep an in-game save before Necrosaro for a dedicated attempt. |

Treat the two restricted Necrosaro achievements as separate clears when planning. A two-person party without the Hero appears to satisfy both written descriptions, but this guide will not depend on both unlocking from one victory. The endgame section will provide a standard clear, a no-Hero composition, and a two-member composition so each condition can be attempted from the same pre-battle save.

The other achievements need attention at their specific locations rather than continuous grinding. Hidden-treasure achievements will have exact local checklists, chapter achievements will be confirmed before the chapter closes, and one-time interactions will be flagged before the route can advance past them.

### Achievement Routing Overview

Achievements are placed in the earliest section where they can be earned without disrupting later preparation. Each walkthrough section begins with an achievement table listing points, the exact trigger, and route notes. Story achievements appear at the event that completes them; treasure achievements appear beside their final required pickup; delayed achievements receive setup notes both when preparation begins and when the unlock becomes available.

- **Achievement Alert: Achievement Name** marks the paragraph where an achievement should unlock.
- **Achievement Setup: Achievement Name (deferred)** marks an action that prepares an achievement earned in a later section.
- Small Medal entries include a running total and identify any collection that becomes unavailable after story progress.
- Hidden-treasure checklists remain separate from ordinary loot so all required pickups can be verified before leaving the location.
- Final-boss challenge achievements use explicit active-party, equipment, tactic, and save-point instructions rather than assuming the normal story party will satisfy them.

### How to Use This Guide

- Follow Chapters 1-4 in order. Their routes are self-contained, but their characters and carried items return in Chapter 5.
- Read each section's achievement table before advancing its story objective. Several achievements depend on optional recruits, interactions, or treasures that are easy to pass.
- Complete every **Achievement Alert** before crossing the section's departure checkpoint. Follow every deferred setup note even though the achievement itself will be repeated at its eventual unlock point.
- Search drawers, jars, conspicuous ground tiles, and dead ends. Ordinary pickups and Small Medals are often outside treasure chests.
- Before ending a solo chapter, convert spare gold into useful equipment or sellable items because loose gold will not join the Chapter 5 treasury.
- Treat tactic recommendations as starting settings, not guarantees. The AI's choices change with party health, enemy knowledge, equipment, and the current battle state.
- Shop recommendations use four tiers: **Required**, **High**, **Situational**, and **Skip**.
- Boss preparation uses the same fields throughout: level, active party, wagon reserves, equipment, consumables, required spells, tactic, recovery point, and opening plan.
- Use each section's return link to jump back to the table of contents before moving to another route segment.

## Chapter 1: Ragnar and the Missing Children

### Section Achievements: Chapter 1

| Achievement | Points | Trigger | Route Notes |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| Reunited | 5 | Help Flora find her missing husband. | Identify Alex in Izmit at night, bring Flora from Burland to his cell, and complete their reunion before entering the Secret Playground. |
| Making Friends | 5 | Let the Healer slime join your party. | Find Healie in the optional eastern branch on the bottom floor of the Secret Playground and accept his offer to join. |
| In Search of a Hero | 10 | Complete Chapter 1. | Rescue the children, return them to Izmit, then report to the King of Burland. |

Chapter 1 follows Ragnar, a soldier of Burland ordered to investigate why children have been disappearing across the kingdom. Ragnar is physically sturdy but slow, has no MP or spells, and fights alone until he finds an optional companion. Equipment and Medical Herb management therefore matter more than complicated battle commands: Ragnar can attack, parry, use an item, or run, but he cannot repair a bad situation with magic on his own.

The chapter's critical path is:

1. Investigate Burland and learn that Flora's husband Alex is missing.
2. Cross the Cave to Izmit and question the villagers by day and night.
3. Identify the prisoner as Alex, bring Flora to him, and learn the location of the Secret Playground.
4. Explore the hidden well, recruit Healie, and recover the Flying Shoes.
5. Use the Flying Shoes beside Loch Tower, rescue the children, and defeat Saro's Shadow.
6. Return the children to Izmit, convert spare gold into carried value, and report to the King of Burland.

### Ragnar's Starting Position

| Resource | Starting Value | Route Implication |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Gold | 50G | Not enough for a meaningful armor upgrade; collect the free herb before buying anything. |
| Weapon | Copper Sword (+12 attack) | Adequate through the Cave to Izmit. Do not replace it with a Club or Thorn Whip. |
| Armor | Leather Armor (+12 defense) | Serviceable for the opening field, but armor upgrades have lasting value because Ragnar's low Agility leaves his Defense equipment-dependent. |
| Magic | None | Carry Medical Herbs and an Antidote Herb until Healie joins. |

Ragnar has high Strength and exceptionally high HP growth, but very low Agility. He usually acts after enemies and gains little natural Defense from Agility, so preventing damage through shields and armor is more reliable than buying every intermediate weapon. His chapter-end gear and carried inventory return with him in Chapter 5.

### Burland Castle and Town

After the opening audience, Ragnar begins in Burland Castle's throne room. The King orders the royal soldiers to discover who is taking the kingdom's children. Before leaving, speak with the people in the castle for the local context, then go to the southeast room on 1F. Search the row of dressers and take the **Medical Herb from the second dresser from the right**. It is hidden rather than displayed as a chest.

Leave the castle for the attached town. Flora stands near the southwest side of town and explains that her husband, Alex, disappeared while looking into the same mystery. Speaking with her now establishes the lead needed later, although Ragnar cannot resolve it until he identifies Alex in Izmit.

The House of Healing near the town entrance is the save point. Its priest can also cure poison, curses, and a defeated character. Make a save before the first field trip. The inn charges 4G, but at the opening budget it is better to take short loops outside and use herbs only when the experience and gold gained justify the cost.

#### Opening Training Loop

Fight near Burland until Ragnar reaches about level 3. Stay close enough to retreat before a bad group can turn into a defeat. Slimes and Stag Beetles are harmless training targets; Giant Worms and Diverats are still manageable but have enough HP to extend a fight. Babbles are the main logistical threat because poison persists after battle. At night, either carry an Antidote Herb or avoid stretching the trip beyond a safe return.

Buy the Leather Shield once Ragnar has 90G, then rest, save, and leave Burland through the northwest route toward the river cave. The exact world-map approach bends west and north around the river; enter the cave that crosses to Izmit's side rather than trying to follow the shoreline directly.

### Cave to Izmit

**Recommended level:** 3  
**Recovery:** Burland before entry; Izmit after exiting  
**Fixed treasure:** Medical Herb, 40G

This is a short one-floor passage and the first chance to learn the guide's dungeon discipline: take side branches when they appear, account for every fixed pickup, and leave before low HP forces an expensive recovery.

1. From the entrance, follow the passage north and turn right at the first available branch.
2. Enter the dark passage to the east. It ends at a chest containing a **Medical Herb**.
3. Backtrack to the main route and continue north into the room with a central pond. The soldier here is lost and does not advance the story.
4. Walk clockwise around the water and take the passage on the left.
5. Follow it north to the chest containing **40G**.
6. Continue north to the exit stairs.

Outside, travel east; Izmit lies a short distance to the north. Healers can prolong cave battles by repeatedly casting Heal. Focus on one target until it falls rather than spreading damage and giving it time to recover.

### Izmit Investigation and the Reunion

Izmit's item shop and inn are near the entrance; its weapon and armor shops are farther north. Search the three dressers in the room on the right side of the inn for a hidden **Medical Herb**. Talk to the adults and children during the day, including the schoolchildren, then inspect the jail below the stairs at the north end of town. The prisoner cannot initially explain who he is.

Stay at the inn or walk outside until night. Return to the schoolyard and speak to the boy moving around the playground. He identifies the prisoner as Alex. Go back underground and speak to the prisoner again so Ragnar can confirm the lead.

Return through the cave to Burland and talk to Flora. She will follow Ragnar without occupying his battle slot. Escort her back through the Cave to Izmit and take her directly to Alex's cell. Flora restores his memory, and Alex explains that the children visit a hidden playground in the forest: from the sign outside Izmit, walk **four steps south and four steps east**.

> **Achievement Alert: Reunited**
>
> **Trigger:** Complete the conversation in Alex's cell after bringing Flora from Burland. The achievement description is "Help Flora find her missing husband."

This event is the progression gate for the Secret Playground. Rest and save in Izmit before following Alex's directions.

### Secret Playground

**Recommended level:** 6  
**Recovery:** Izmit before and after the trip  
**Fixed treasure:** 2 Medical Herbs, 600G, Agility Seed, Flying Shoes  
**Optional recruit:** Healie

Walk to the sign outside Izmit, count four tiles south and four tiles east, and enter the hidden forest clearing. Walk north and descend the well. A voice gives directions through the passages. Following it leads safely toward the objective and suppresses most encounters; leaving its route leads to the optional treasure and Healie. This guide deliberately leaves the safe path to clear the area.

#### B1: First Branches

1. Follow the initial passage south.
2. At the first split, continue south as the voice directs.
3. At the T intersection, go west.
4. At the next split, go south and ignore the staircase to the north for now.
5. Continue until the voice speaks again. Ignore it and go east into the side room.
6. Follow the winding passage to its dead end and open the chest for a **Medical Herb**.
7. Return to the last intersection, go south, and descend to B2.

#### B2: Recruit Healie Before Taking the Shoes

From the stairs, walk east. When the voice tells Ragnar to turn, ignore it and continue all the way east to the dead end. Speak to the Healer slime there and answer **Yes** when he offers to join.

> **Achievement Alert: Making Friends**
>
> **Trigger:** Accept Healie into the party. The achievement description is "Let the Healer slime join your party."

Healie is optional for story completion but central to the safe route. He acts independently, has a basic physical attack, and casts Heal on Ragnar during battle. He also gives enemies a second target. Ragnar still needs Medical Herbs because Healie can spend all of his MP or wait longer than desired to heal.

Retrace the passage to the voice, then take the northern route. Continue north through the next intersection. At the following T split, go east against the voice's direction and follow the long passage to the chest containing **600G**. This money should fund most or all of the Iron Shield, depending on earlier spending.

Return to that T split and take the western branch recommended by the voice. Open the chest on the platform for the **Flying Shoes**. This key item is required to enter Loch Tower.

#### Exit Route and Hidden Pots

Return to the stairs in the southwest corner of B2 and climb to B1. Go north, west, north, then east to reach the staircase that was bypassed earlier. Follow the zigzag passage toward the entrance. Near the first split, take the western branch into the room with cracked flooring and deliberately fall through the opening.

After landing, climb the nearby stairs into the small room with five pots. Search all five to collect a **Medical Herb** and an **Agility Seed**, then use the eastern staircase to emerge near the well. Walk southeast around the trees and leave the clearing to the west.

### Loch Tower

**Recommended level:** 8-9 on entry; about 9-10 for the boss  
**Required item:** Flying Shoes  
**Recovery:** Unlimited full HP/MP restoration on the basement recovery tile  
**Fixed treasure:** Wing of Wyvern, Strength Seed, 640G, Scale Shield, Luck Seed, Sword of Malice

Walk west from Izmit to the water surrounding Loch Tower. Stand beside the moat and **use** the Flying Shoes from Ragnar's inventory. Ragnar and Healie land on the tower's roof, where they see a child being led below. The supplied guides count the roof as either the entry level or 4F, so this route identifies floors by sequence and landmark as well as number.

#### Roof to 2F

1. Descend from the roof and enter the northern passage.
2. Take the left branch to the dead-end chest containing a **Wing of Wyvern**.
3. Return to the entry stairs, take the southern route, then walk east past the soldier.
4. Descend the stairs at the end to reach 2F.
5. From the 2F stairs, walk north past the large door and climb the northeast stairs.
6. On the upper landing, open the nearby chest for a **Strength Seed**, then return to 2F.
7. Enter the large room on 2F and open the chest for **640G**.
8. Continue clockwise around the floor. At the pair of large pillars, walk north between them into the covered room and take the **Scale Shield**.
9. Continue north and descend to 1F.

#### 1F Treasure and Recovery Hub

From the 1F stairs, first go south and descend to the basement. Step onto the white tile at the north end to restore Ragnar and Healie to full HP and MP. The tile can be used repeatedly, making this the best place in the chapter to finish any required leveling.

Return to 1F and open the large door north of the stairs. The eastern branch leads to a chest containing a **Luck Seed**. The mirrored western branch leads to the **Sword of Malice**. Equip it immediately: its +45 attack is 17 points stronger than the Iron Spear and 25 stronger than the Chain Sickle. When used as an item in battle, it produces a group-targeting Firebal effect, making it useful against groups or enemies whose Defense has been raised by Upper.

Keep both seeds unused for Chapter 5 allocation. Heal again after collecting the weapon if the detours cost meaningful HP or MP.

### Returning the Children

After the battle, the children follow Ragnar. Backtrack to the tower's ground-level front entrance and step outside. The moat prevents a direct walk to Izmit. If Ragnar still has the Wing of Wyvern, use it to return immediately. Otherwise, use the Flying Shoes outside the tower to return to the roof, then walk off the south edge; Ragnar, Healie, and the children land safely beyond the moat.

Enter Izmit to reunite the children with their families. Random encounters cease for the return to Burland, so this is the chapter's final preparation window rather than another combat leg.

Before speaking to the King, spend nearly all remaining gold. Loose gold does not carry into Chapter 5, while Ragnar's equipped gear and carried items do. Prioritize the permanent set in this order:

1. Half Plate Armor if Ragnar does not own it.
2. Iron Shield if Ragnar does not own it.
3. Wooden Hat if Ragnar does not own it.
4. Extra equipment or consumables that retain resale or practical value in Chapter 5.

Keep the Sword of Malice equipped and retain the Strength, Agility, and Luck Seeds. Do not sell unique or useful carried items merely to reach exactly zero gold. The objective is to avoid abandoning currency, not to weaken Ragnar's returning inventory.

Leave Izmit, cross the connecting cave back to Burland, enter the castle, and approach the throne. Ragnar reports what the kidnappers were seeking and declares his intention to find and protect the Hero. The King awards Ragnar **3,000 EXP**, normally raising him to approximately level 12 or 13, and Chapter 1 ends.

## Chapter 2: Alena's Journey to Endor

### Section Achievements: Chapter 2

Chapter 2 follows Princess Alena of Santeem, whose desire to see the world is stronger than her father's order to remain inside the castle. Unlike Ragnar's solo opening, this chapter quickly forms a balanced three-person party: Alena supplies fast physical damage, Cristo supplies healing and defensive magic, and Brey supplies attack magic and enemy debuffs.

The chapter's critical path is:

1. Break out of Santeem Castle and meet Cristo and Brey outside.
2. Equip and train the party around Surene before traveling to Tempe.
3. Volunteer as Tempe's offering and defeat the monsters threatening the village.
4. Investigate the false princess in Frenor, recover the Golden Bracelet, and exchange it for May and the Thief's Key.
5. Visit the Bazaar, answer Santeem's summons, and trace the cure for the King's lost voice to Birdsong Tower.
6. Restore the King's voice, cross the travel-door shrine to Endor, and prepare Alena for the solo tournament.
7. Win five consecutive matches, return to the now-empty Santeem Castle, and leave to close the chapter.

**Starting level:** 1

## Chapter 3: Taloon's Road to Riches

### Section Achievements: Chapter 3

Chapter 3 follows Taloon, an employee in Lakanaba's weapon shop who wants to own a store of his own. His route is built around commerce: working for wages, recognizing valuable merchandise, selling armor where demand is highest, recovering a rare statuette, and using his own shop to turn inventory into the money needed for two major investments.

Taloon has no spells and begins far less combat-ready than Ragnar or Alena. He compensates with high HP growth, broad equipment access, unusually frequent monster drops during his chapter, and random special actions in battle. Those actions cannot be selected directly, so the safe route still depends on armor, Medical Herbs, and optional hired companions.

The chapter's critical path is:

1. Work at Lakanaba's weapon shop, buy basic equipment, and learn about the Iron Safe.
2. Solve the Cave North of Lakanaba and leave with the Iron Safe.
3. Visit Foxville and Bonmalmo, free Grandpa Tom's son, and borrow his dog Tov.
4. Expose Foxville, free the architect Da Gardi, and reopen the bridge to Endor.
5. Deliver the Prince's Letter and Royal Scroll to prevent the war and earn permission to own a shop.
6. Recover and sell the Silver Statuette, then buy the vacant Endor shop for 35,000G.
7. Deliver seven Broad Swords and seven Half Plate Armors to the King for 60,000G.
8. Give the 60,000G to the tunnel builder, wait for construction to finish, and cross to Branca.

### Lakanaba: Working Capital

Work roughly two days, or until Taloon can buy a Thorn Whip and basic armor. The owner normally pays about 100G per completed shift. Sleep at home for free, accept the next Lunch from Neta, and repeat only as long as the guaranteed upgrades are useful.

### Cave North of Lakanaba

**Recommended level:** 3-4  
**Fixed treasure:** Chain Sickle, Iron Safe  
**Critical warning:** Bring a movable boulder into the Iron Safe room before taking the chest.

Puzzle rooms in this cave do not generate random encounters, so take time to position the boulders correctly. The monsters elsewhere are the same low-tier enemies found around Lakanaba.

#### Reservoir Route

1. From the entrance, walk west and descend the stairs.
2. Follow the linear passage until it reaches two stairways; both connect to the same route.
3. Examine the barrier holding back the first reservoir. The released water carries Taloon down to the next stairs, and this first mechanism resets when revisited.
4. Continue until reaching the second reservoir barrier.
5. Before opening it, walk west past the barrier and then north to the chest containing the **Chain Sickle**. Equip it immediately.
6. Return to the barrier and release the second reservoir. The water carries Taloon to the floor below; unlike the first mechanism, this change remains in place.

#### Rolling Boulder Puzzle

From the landing, walk north and take the western branch, then descend at its end. Ignore the nearby southern stairs and continue west to the wall holding a large boulder. Stand below the barrier and walk south to release it.

The boulder advances whenever Taloon moves. Keep it close enough to follow the turns without letting it roll onto the wrong line. Move east into the next corridor, south at the bend, then west at the first available branch. Continue to the western wall and step north. The boulder rolls south into the pit, creating a bridge. Cross it and descend to the bottom floor.

If the boulder misses the pit, use the nearby stairs to reset the puzzle room and try again. No treasure is lost.

#### Iron Safe Trap

Two small movable boulders are on the bottom floor. Use the leftmost one.

1. Push the boulder north along the passage toward the room containing the gray chest.
2. Stop with the boulder inside the room and directly south of the chest before touching the treasure.
3. Open the chest and take the **Iron Safe**. Removing it raises a wall over the doorway and reveals a floor switch where the chest stood.
4. Push the boulder north onto that switch. The wall lowers and Taloon can leave.

If Taloon takes the Iron Safe without bringing a boulder into the room, he cannot reach a boulder after the wall closes. Do not save after trapping him; reset and repeat the bottom-floor puzzle.

Retrace the cave route and step outside with the Iron Safe still in Taloon's inventory.

### Cave of the Silver Statuette

**Recommended level:** 11  
**Fixed treasure:** Wing of Wyvern, Morning Star, 760G, Medical Herb, Iron Spear, Broad Sword, Half Plate Armor, Silver Statuette  
**Required action:** Drain the upper reservoir to expose the lower routes

Clear inventory space before entering. Taloon has only eight personal item slots, and the Iron Safe plus equipped gear consume several of them. The route identifies two valuable chests that can deliberately remain unopened for Chapter 5.

#### Entry and Drainage Switch

1. Follow the entrance passage until reaching stairs beside a boat. Ignore the stairs and board the boat.
2. Follow the waterway into the dark opening and fall with the boat to the lower flooded level.
3. Disembark on the northern landing. Take the western branch to the chest containing a **Wing of Wyvern**.
4. Return east, continue to the far wall, then turn south around the raised platform.
5. Take the stairs in the southeast corner to the reservoir-control floor.
6. Follow the northern route and turn west at the split. Continue north to the floor switch.
7. Stand on the switch and answer **Yes**. The water drains from this floor and floods the level below, opening both its boat route and the remaining treasure ledges.

#### Drained-Floor Treasure

Backtrack from the switch and collect:

- **Morning Star** from the chest near the western return route. Taloon cannot equip it, so reserve it for sale.
- **760G** from the northern room.
- **Medical Herb** from the far southern chest.
- **Iron Spear** from the northeast chest.

The nearby western stairs provide the quickest exit to the cave entrance after the statuette is secured.

#### Flooded-Level Treasure and the Statuette

Descend the newly exposed southeast stairs and board the waiting boat. Sail north, turn west, pass the opening to the north, and continue south into the isolated room containing a **Broad Sword**. The chest in the northwest portion of the flooded level contains **Half Plate Armor**.

The two adjacent chests in the top-right area of B3 are empty. They have separate opened flags but contain no items, so the efficient route can skip them.

Neither item is usable by Taloon. The long-term route leaves both chests unopened so the Chapter 5 party can collect the +40 Broad Sword and +30 Half Plate Armor directly. If Taloon is still well short of 35,000G and the player prefers less grinding now, collecting and selling them is valid, but it exchanges future equipment for only 2,400G of normal resale value.

Return to the boat and sail into the north-central room. Land on the decorated platform, descend the stairs, and follow the final passage north to the chest containing the **Silver Statuette**. Backtrack to the drained-floor western stairs and leave the cave. A Wing of Wyvern can shorten the return to Endor once Taloon is outside.

### Selling the Statuette and Buying the Shop

Return to the collector upstairs in Endor's northeast house and sell the Silver Statuette for **25,000G**. Taloon now needs another 10,000G.

#### Reliable Profit Loop

Neta sells merchandise above normal retail value. Use the following loop regardless of whether Lakanaba stocked a Sword of Malice:

1. Give Neta one or more valuable weapons or armor pieces from behind the counter.
2. Leave Endor and remain outside until night.
3. Return to the shop and sleep upstairs.
4. Speak with Neta from behind the counter the next morning. First complete the merchandise prompt, then collect the previous day's proceeds.
5. Reinvest the profit into additional order pieces or high-value merchandise and repeat.

If the Sword of Malice entered Lakanaba's stock, buy as many 3,500G copies as Taloon can carry. Neta commonly sells each for roughly 5,000G or more, making this the fastest loop. If it never appeared, use Broad Swords, Half Plate Armors, fixed cave loot, and monster drops instead. The guaranteed route is slower but does not depend on a random customer.

The statuette cave remains a good source of both battles and equipment drops. Preserve the unopened Broad Sword and Half Plate Armor for Chapter 5 if possible; the royal order can be filled entirely with purchased copies, and unopened treasure remains available to the reunited party.

Deliver the order in batches as inventory fills. When the seventh sword and seventh armor are accepted, the castle pays Taloon **60,000G**. Do not spend this payment: it is exactly the amount required for the tunnel.

### Final Inventory Conversion

Once the tunnel is complete, Taloon no longer needs combat equipment in this chapter. Ordinary gold will not transfer to the united party, but carried equipment will. Convert remaining profits before crossing the tunnel.

The maximum-value route uses the seven inventory slots not occupied by the Iron Safe:

- If available, buy **seven Swords of Malice** for 24,500G total. Their normal resale value is 2,625G each, giving the Chapter 5 party 18,375G of portable value.
- Otherwise, buy **seven Broad Swords** for 14,000G total. Their normal resale value is 1,500G each, giving the Chapter 5 party 10,500G.
- If Taloon will be used immediately when recruited, keep his best equipment instead of maximizing cargo. The recommended equipped set is Abacus of Virtue / Iron Apron / Iron Shield / Wooden Hat.

Only strip Taloon's equipment after all Chapter 3 combat is complete. Any gold left after filling his inventory can be converted into casino coins, but the 200G rate makes this a last resort rather than the primary carryover plan.

Chapter 5 begins the continuous main quest. The Hero starts alone at level 1, while the characters developed during Chapters 1-4 retain their levels and ordinary personal equipment until they rejoin. The section's critical path is:

1. Complete the opening events in the hidden village and salvage its remaining items.
2. Equip and level the Hero around the Woodman's Shack and Branca.
3. Cross Taloon's completed tunnel to Endor and complete the delayed `Tunnel Rat` setup.

5. Recover any useful Chapter 3 treasure left in the Silver Statuette cave or purchase equivalent Hero equipment.
6. Return through Branca and meet Hector at the Desert Inn.

### The Hero's Hometown

**Starting level:** 1  
**Starting equipment:** Copper Sword, Basic Clothes  
**Fixed pickups:** Medical Herb, Feather Hat

### Necrosaro's Mountain

With the wagon present and the desired active formation selected, walk north into the mountain. The ascent is a short winding path with occasional Flamadog encounters. Preserve HP and MP rather than grinding here. At the summit, approach Necrosaro to begin one continuous seven-form battle.

There is no recovery break, inventory access, or save opportunity between forms. Each transformation receives a new HP pool, for a total of **5,696 HP** across the battle.

### Boss: Necrosaro

| Form | HP | Actions and Threats | Primary Response |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| 1 | 800 | Two physical actions per round. | Establish a sustainable healing rhythm while physical attackers begin dealing damage. |
| 2 | 650 | One physical action per round after losing an arm. | Use the reduced pressure to restore HP and prepare for spellcasting forms. |
| 3 | 1,023 | Firebane, Snowstorm, Increase, and sweet breath. | Remove Increase with the Zenithian Sword's Disperse effect when practical; use Awake after sleep. |
| 4 | 700 | Freezing wave, scorching gas, violent blaze, and Meditation. | Keep attacking through Meditation and avoid overinvesting in buffs that the wave removes. |
| 5 | 800 | Two actions and party-wide scorching gas. | Use the Sage's Stone regularly and keep fragile characters above danger range. |
| 6 | 700 | Continues the two-action physical and gas pressure. | Recover toward full HP before forcing the final transformation. |
| 7 | 1,023 | Opens with Bounce, acts twice, uses violent blaze, icy breath, and freezing wave. | Stop offensive spellcasting, use free group healing repeatedly, and finish with physical damage. |

Necrosaro awards no EXP or gold. The achievement triggers are tied to defeating the final form, not to an end-of-battle reward screen.

#### Forms 1 and 2: The Arms

Begin on Normal tactics. Form 1 attacks twice, so use the Sage's Stone whenever more than one character needs meaningful healing. Alena and Ragnar should attack continuously while Cristo supports them. If using Bikill, apply it to the strongest physical attacker, but do not sacrifice urgent recovery for setup.

After the first arm is destroyed, Form 2 acts only once per round. This is the safest part of the battle. Restore the party, revive anyone who fell, and stabilize MP before removing the second arm. Avoid rushing the transformation while a healer is weak.

#### Form 3: Head and Magic

Without both arms, Necrosaro turns to Firebane, Snowstorm, Increase, and sweet breath. If the Hero is active, use the Zenithian Sword as an item to remove Increase when the defense boost materially slows the attackers. Cast Awake when sleep disables an essential healer or damage dealer.

Barrier helps against spell damage if the AI supplies it, but remember that later forms erase support effects. Keep direct damage moving and preserve enough healing for the more dangerous transformations.

#### Form 4: The First Transformation

After losing his original head, Necrosaro becomes a green creature with a new face. He can erase active spell effects with a freezing wave, recover HP through Meditation, and damage the party with scorching gas or violent blaze.

Do not enter a long cycle of rebuilding every removed buff. Heal first, restore only high-value effects when the party has breathing room, and pressure the 700 HP pool so Meditation cannot prolong the form indefinitely.

#### Forms 5 and 6: Arms and Legs Restored

Form 5 regrows its arms and returns to two actions per round. Scorching gas can damage the entire active party before a healer responds. Use the Sage's Stone whenever group HP is uneven and reserve Healusall for rounds where free healing will not be enough.

Form 6 adds the legs but follows the same basic pattern. Near the end of its 700 HP pool, slow down long enough to restore the party. The next form opens with Bounce and has the battle's highest sustained group-damage potential.

#### Form 7: Final Form

The final form begins with Bounce, acts twice, uses violent blaze and icy breath, and can erase support effects with the freezing wave. Do not let Mara, Brey, or another caster repeatedly throw reflected attack spells. Physical attackers should carry the offense while the Hero uses the Sage's Stone on most damaged rounds.

Treat a freezing wave that removes few or no active buffs as a low-damage opportunity. Attack aggressively on that turn, but keep enough HP to survive two consecutive breath attacks. If Cristo is present, Defensive tactics can improve recovery consistency when the party falls behind.

Defeating the 1,023 HP final form ends the battle and begins Necrosaro's collapse.

> **Achievement Alert: Is This The End?**
>
> **Trigger:** Defeat Necrosaro's seventh and final form.

> **Achievement Alert: We Don't Need Another Hero**
>
> **Trigger:** Defeat Necrosaro while the Hero remains outside the active party. Keep the Hero in the wagon for the complete battle to satisfy the condition cleanly.

> **Achievement Alert: Just the Two of Us**
>
> **Trigger:** Defeat Necrosaro with exactly two active party members. Keep all other characters and Doran out of the active formation for the complete battle.

The normal achievement unlocks on every successful clear. The two challenge alerts apply only when their stated formation was maintained. Reload the Last Refuge save after the ending to make another attempt; unlocked RetroAchievements remain credited even though the game itself returns to the pre-boss save.

### The Ending

Necrosaro's mountain begins to collapse after the battle. The Master Dragon rescues the party and returns everyone to Zenithia, where fallen allies are restored and the assembled heroes are thanked for ending the threat.

Explore Zenithia for the residents' final dialogue if desired, then leave the castle to commit to the ending sequence. The balloon carries the companions home in turn:

1. Alena, Cristo, and Brey return to the restored Santeem Castle.
2. Ragnar returns to Burland.
3. Taloon reunites with his family in Endor.
4. Nara and Mara visit Kievs and then return to Monbaraba.
5. The Hero returns alone to the ruins of Hometown.

At Hometown, Celia reappears. Ragnar, Alena, Cristo, Brey, Taloon, Nara, and Mara then arrive to reunite with the Hero before the credits.

There is no playable postgame after the credits. Use the preserved Last Refuge save for any remaining achievement cleanup or additional Necrosaro formations.

### High Roller: Endor Casino

**Location:** Basement beneath the Endor inn  
**Chapter 5 exchange rate:** 20G per Casino coin  
**Required balance:** 10,000 coins at one time  
**Recommended game:** 100-coin poker

Casino coins are shared across chapters, so any balance retained from Alena or Taloon reduces the Chapter 5 grind. Speak with the exchange attendant to buy a starting reserve. The casino offers slot machines, poker, and the Monster Arena. Slots and the arena provide less control over an individual attempt. Poker supports 1-, 10-, and 100-coin antes and gives access to a double-or-nothing sequence after a winning hand, making it the most practical route to a five-digit balance.

#### Poker and Double-or-Nothing Route

1. Record the adventure normally in Endor before beginning the session.
2. Buy enough coins to absorb several maximum-ante hands without immediately reaching zero.
3. Use the 100-coin poker table once the reserve can support normal losing streaks.
4. After a winning hand, choose double-or-nothing and predict whether the next card will be higher or lower.
5. Each correct prediction doubles the current award. Collect after a meaningful gain rather than risking the entire chain indefinitely.
6. Leave the casino and make another normal in-game save after reaching a new balance milestone.
7. Reload the normal save after a major losing session, then resume from the banked balance.

### Permanent Character Reference

Chapter 5 permits direct commands only for the Hero. Every other active character follows the selected tactic and develops resistance knowledge through repeated encounters. Party advice therefore considers both a character's abilities and how reliably the AI uses them.

| Character | Class or Role | Strengths | Limitations | Primary Endgame Use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hero | Balanced fighter and spellcaster | Strong equipment, physical damage, lightning magic, healing, and direct player control | Must divide turns among damage, healing, items, and utility | Sage's Stone controller, emergency healer, and flexible attacker |
| Ragnar | Soldier and physical tank | Very high HP, strength, defense, and heavy-equipment access | Low agility and no spells | Durable sustained damage with the Metal Babble Sword |
| Alena | Fast physical attacker | Very high agility, frequent critical hits, and strong damage with Stilleto Earrings | Limited equipment, moderate defense, and no spells | Boss damage and King Metal hunting |
| Cristo | Defensive mage and primary healer | Healall, Healus, Revive, Increase, and strong late defensive equipment | AI can waste turns on Beat or Defeat until enemy resistance is learned | Main AI-controlled healer and revival support |
| Brey | Offensive and support wizard | Ice magic, Bikill, SpeedUp, Defense, Bounce, and Transform | Low HP and defense; high-level magic consumes MP quickly | Physical-party support and ice damage |
| Taloon | Merchant and utility fighter | High HP, broad equipment access, and useful autonomous special actions | Slower and less predictable than Ragnar or Alena; no spells | Durable reserve attacker and challenge-party substitute |
| Nara | Defensive mage and secondary healer | Healall, Barrier, Vivify, status recovery, and Infernos-family damage | Low HP and physical damage; Farewell removes her from battle | Backup healer and breath-defense support |
| Mara | Offensive mage | Fire, explosion, Sap, RobMagic, BeDragon, and high MP | Low physical durability and AI spell choices can be inefficient | Group damage, boss spell pressure, and metal hunting |

### Tactics Quick Reference

| Tactic | Best Use | Main Risk |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Normal | Default for exploration and unfamiliar bosses | May not emphasize healing or damage strongly enough during emergencies. |
| Offensive | Fast clears and encouraging Mara's strongest attacks or BeDragon | Rapid MP use and delayed support. |
| Defensive | Long bosses and AI-controlled challenge parties needing frequent recovery | Fighters may defend instead of finishing a vulnerable target. |
| Save MP | Routine travel when resources must last | Suppresses costly group healing and BeDragon. |
| Use No MP | Weak encounters and physical-only cleanup | Prevents emergency spell recovery. |
| Try Out | Situational experiments with unusual actions or items | Deliberately unpredictable and unsafe for serious bosses. |
